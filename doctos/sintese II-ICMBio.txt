# Biodiversity of Streams in and Around the Guaribas Biological Reserve: Threats and Conservation

Good morning, and thank you for the opportunity to present this work.

Today I will discuss the biodiversity of streams within the **Guaribas Biological Reserve (REBIO Guaribas)** and its surrounding areas, focusing on the ecological integrity of these freshwater ecosystems, the threats they currently face, and the implications for conservation.

This presentation synthesizes more than a decade of research conducted by the Ecology Laboratory of the State University of Paraíba, involving studies on fish assemblages, zooplankton communities, benthic macroinvertebrates, environmental quality, and physiological biomarkers of environmental stress.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Introduction

The Atlantic Forest is one of the world's most important biodiversity hotspots. Despite occupying only a small fraction of its original distribution, it continues to support extraordinary levels of species richness and endemism. At the same time, it is one of the most threatened tropical ecosystems on Earth due to habitat loss, fragmentation, urban expansion, and agricultural development.

Conservation efforts in the Atlantic Forest have traditionally focused on terrestrial organisms such as plants, birds, and mammals. However, freshwater ecosystems are equally dependent on forest integrity and are often overlooked in conservation planning.

Streams are strongly connected to surrounding terrestrial habitats through ecological processes that regulate energy flow, nutrient cycling, habitat structure, and food-web dynamics. Many stream fishes rely heavily on allochthonous inputs such as terrestrial insects, fruits, seeds, and leaf litter. Riparian vegetation also regulates water temperature, stabilizes stream banks, reduces sediment input, and maintains water quality.

Consequently, the conservation of freshwater biodiversity cannot be separated from the conservation of forest ecosystems.

Furthermore, stream fauna can serve as a highly sensitive indicator of environmental change. Because aquatic organisms respond rapidly to habitat degradation, pollution, and hydrological alteration, they provide valuable information about ecosystem health and ecological integrity.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Objectives

The primary objectives of this research program were:

1.  To inventory aquatic fauna within REBIO Guaribas and surrounding areas.
2.  To characterize patterns of biodiversity in local stream ecosystems.
3.  To identify major threats affecting ecological integrity.
4.  To evaluate evidence of environmental degradation using ecological and physiological indicators.
5.  To provide scientific information that can support conservation and management strategies.

Ultimately, the goal is to contribute to the long-term conservation of Atlantic Forest freshwater ecosystems.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Study Area

REBIO Guaribas is located in northeastern Brazil, within the Atlantic Forest domain of Paraíba State.

An important characteristic of this protected area is its position within a watershed divide. Streams originating inside and around the reserve do not follow political or administrative boundaries. Water, sediments, nutrients, contaminants, and organisms move throughout the drainage network regardless of protected-area limits.

This means that activities occurring outside the reserve can directly influence ecological processes occurring inside the reserve.

Therefore, understanding the condition of streams both within and beyond the boundaries of REBIO Guaribas is essential for effective conservation planning.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Research Framework and Sampling

Research was conducted in two main phases.

The first phase involved repeated sampling at fixed stream sites between February 2011 and January 2012.

The second phase expanded the study area to include streams located in the surrounding landscape between May and July 2013.

Subsequent projects continued evaluating aquatic biodiversity and environmental quality through studies of fish assemblages, zooplankton communities, macroinvertebrate colonization, and physiological biomarkers.

Environmental measurements included:

-   Stream morphology
-   Habitat structure
-   Substrate composition
-   Water quality variables
-   Dissolved oxygen
-   Nutrient concentrations
-   Temperature
-   Flow velocity

Biological sampling targeted:

-   Fish communities
-   Zooplankton
-   Benthic macroinvertebrates

In addition, physiological analyses were conducted to evaluate environmental stress responses in aquatic organisms.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Environmental Characteristics of the Streams

The streams studied are typical headwater systems.

Most are relatively narrow and shallow, with widths ranging from approximately one to more than one hundred meters and average marginal depths around fifty centimeters.

Water velocity is generally low to moderate.

Substrate composition is dominated by sand and fine sediments, although habitat complexity varies considerably among sites.

Within well-preserved sections, riparian vegetation contributes leaf litter, roots, woody debris, and shading that create structurally complex habitats.

However, outside the reserve, habitat degradation becomes increasingly evident.

Several streams show reduced riparian cover, increased light penetration, sediment accumulation, and altered channel conditions.

Dissolved oxygen concentrations also reveal important contrasts.

Some streams exhibit oxygen levels consistent with healthy aquatic environments, whereas others show concentrations indicative of environmental stress and reduced habitat quality.

These observations suggest that although stream ecosystems within the reserve remain relatively well preserved, surrounding areas experience substantial anthropogenic pressure.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Nutrient Dynamics and Water Quality

Recent analyses of nutrient concentrations provide further evidence of environmental impacts.

Ammonia concentrations varied considerably among sites, with some streams exhibiting elevated values.

Ammonia is particularly important because it can directly affect aquatic organisms by disrupting physiological processes and, under extreme conditions, causing mortality.

Nitrate concentrations were generally lower, but elevated nutrient inputs may still contribute to eutrophication and broader ecosystem changes.

The spatial distribution of nutrient concentrations suggests that streams outside protected areas are more strongly influenced by human activities.

Potential sources include agriculture, livestock production, domestic wastewater, and land-use changes throughout the watershed.

Although nutrient levels do not yet indicate severe degradation throughout the system, they represent an important early warning signal.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Fish Diversity

Fish communities represent one of the most important components of stream biodiversity.

Sampling across seventeen sites revealed a remarkably diverse ichthyofauna.

A total of eighteen fish species were recorded, representing seven families and five taxonomic orders.

Characiformes was the most diverse order, while Characidae was the richest family.

Notably, species richness within the study area is comparable to that reported for much larger regional river systems.

This finding highlights the conservation value of these relatively small Atlantic Forest streams.

Several species occurred only once during sampling, indicating the presence of rare or spatially restricted populations.

Others were recorded at only two sites, demonstrating considerable heterogeneity among streams.

Such patterns suggest that biodiversity conservation requires protection of multiple habitats throughout the watershed rather than a few isolated locations.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Invasive Species

Despite the overall conservation value of the fish fauna, invasive species were also detected.

Among the most notable examples were Nile tilapia (*Oreochromis niloticus*) and guppy (*Poecilia reticulata*).

These species are widely recognized as successful invaders capable of altering ecological interactions, competing with native species, modifying habitats, and changing food-web structure.

Their presence highlights the vulnerability of stream ecosystems to biological invasions, particularly when environmental conditions become altered through habitat degradation or hydrological modification.

The spread of invasive species represents a significant conservation concern because native stream fishes are often characterized by small body size, restricted distributions, and specialized habitat requirements.

Such characteristics increase their susceptibility to local extinction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Zooplankton as Indicators of Environmental Change

Zooplankton communities provide another valuable perspective on ecosystem condition.

Studies conducted in streams near REBIO Guaribas demonstrated clear associations between community composition and environmental quality.

Patterns observed in zooplankton assemblages suggest the presence of organic pollution and modification of riparian vegetation in streams located outside the reserve.

Because zooplankton respond rapidly to nutrient enrichment and habitat alteration, these organisms function as effective bioindicators of environmental change.

The results reinforce the idea that impacts occurring in surrounding landscapes are influencing aquatic ecosystems connected to the reserve.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Macroinvertebrate Communities

Benthic macroinvertebrates are among the most widely used indicators of stream health worldwide.

Experimental studies evaluating colonization of artificial substrates revealed important relationships between habitat quality and community composition.

A total of twenty-four macroinvertebrate families were recorded.

The strongest predictors of community structure were the presence of leaf litter and organic matter.

Streams with intact riparian vegetation provided a continuous supply of organic material, creating favorable conditions for macroinvertebrate diversity and abundance.

Conversely, sediment deposition had negative effects on aquatic insect communities.

Excessive sedimentation can bury habitat, reduce oxygen availability, and alter resource availability, ultimately reducing biodiversity.

These results emphasize the critical ecological role of riparian forests.

By preventing erosion and maintaining organic matter inputs, riparian vegetation supports the ecological processes that sustain aquatic communities.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Physiological Biomarkers and Environmental Stress

Ecological responses often become visible only after environmental impacts have already affected populations and communities.

For this reason, physiological biomarkers can provide particularly valuable early-warning indicators.

One biomarker evaluated in this research was the Multixenobiotic Resistance mechanism, or MXR.

MXR functions as a cellular defense system against pollutants.

When organisms encounter potentially harmful substances—including pesticides, heavy metals, pharmaceuticals, and microplastics—they activate transport proteins that remove toxic compounds from cells.

This mechanism reduces intracellular accumulation of contaminants and protects cellular integrity.

Measurements of MXR activity revealed higher values in organisms collected outside the reserve.

Increased activity indicates greater exposure to environmental contaminants and suggests that aquatic organisms in surrounding streams are experiencing elevated chemical stress.

These findings provide physiological evidence supporting patterns already observed through environmental measurements and community-level analyses.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Integrated Threat Assessment

Taken together, the results reveal a consistent pattern.

Multiple threats are acting simultaneously across the landscape.

These include:

-   Destruction of headwater areas
-   Riparian forest degradation
-   Sedimentation
-   Water pollution
-   Agricultural chemicals
-   Hydrological modification
-   Biological invasions

Importantly, these threats do not operate independently.

Instead, they interact to create a complex system of environmental degradation that progressively reduces ecological integrity.

Loss of riparian vegetation increases erosion.

Increased erosion promotes sedimentation.

Sedimentation alters habitat structure.

Habitat degradation favors invasive species.

Pollution increases physiological stress.

Together, these processes undermine ecosystem resilience and biodiversity conservation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Future Challenges

Future scenarios present additional concerns.

Climate change is expected to alter precipitation regimes and increase hydrological variability throughout northeastern Brazil.

At the same time, urban growth and agricultural expansion continue to increase demand for freshwater resources.

Potential consequences include:

-   Greater competition for water use
-   Construction of dams and water withdrawals
-   Reduced stream flow
-   Depletion of headwater systems
-   Declining water quality
-   Loss of ecosystem services

These pressures may further amplify existing threats and compromise the long-term persistence of freshwater biodiversity.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Conservation Implications

Several important conservation lessons emerge from this research.

First, REBIO Guaribas continues to protect a highly valuable freshwater ecosystem.

The reserve supports diverse fish assemblages, rich aquatic communities, and habitats that remain relatively well preserved.

Second, ecological integrity inside the reserve depends strongly on conditions outside the reserve.

Protected-area boundaries alone are insufficient to ensure conservation of aquatic ecosystems.

Because streams connect landscapes through hydrological processes, degradation occurring in surrounding areas can directly affect biodiversity within the reserve.

Third, conservation strategies must prioritize watershed-scale management.

Protecting headwaters, restoring riparian vegetation, reducing pollution inputs, controlling invasive species, and maintaining natural hydrological processes are essential actions.

Monitoring programs should also be expanded to detect environmental changes before irreversible biodiversity losses occur.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Conclusions

In conclusion, the streams of REBIO Guaribas harbor an exceptionally important component of Atlantic Forest biodiversity.

Fish, zooplankton, macroinvertebrates, and other aquatic organisms reveal the ecological value of these ecosystems and their dependence on intact riparian habitats.

At the same time, evidence from environmental measurements, community composition, and physiological biomarkers demonstrates that surrounding landscapes are already exerting measurable impacts on aquatic ecosystems.

The future conservation of these streams will depend not only on protecting the reserve itself, but also on preserving and restoring the broader watershed in which it is embedded.

Ultimately, conserving the biodiversity of REBIO Guaribas requires protecting both the forest and the water that sustains it.

Thank you very much for your attention.
